History

1890 Sewerage system completed.
2004 The Chandelier Hall designated as an archaeological monument (No. 464) and listed for preservation.

Description

The Kronleuchtersaal (chandelier Hall) is a rather curious chamber in Cologne’s sewer system, situated beneath the Theodor-Heuss-Ring. Where several tunnels intersect, there is a large chamber with a barrel vault. Technically speaking, it is a junction structure that connects the northern ring road collector – which collects wastewater from the area around the Kölner Ringe – with the Cleverstraße interceptor. It remains part of the sewerage system to this day and is therefore technical infrastructure. For this reason, Stadtentwässerungsbetriebe Köln (StEB) is responsible for it and offers public guided tours. The site is also used for concerts.

The name refers to its most striking feature: two large chandeliers hanging from the ceiling in the centre of the hall. Not exactly standard fare for a sewer system. The city of Cologne was expanded at the end of the 19th century, and the medieval city walls were demolished in 1881. Naturally, the New Town also required infrastructure such as water and sewerage, and so this junction was built to connect the new sewerage system, which was developed under the direction of City Planning Officer Carl Steuernagel. The sewerage system was advanced. To mark its inauguration, two chandeliers and several wall sconces were installed here, and they have never been removed. Why these particular chandeliers were chosen and where they came from is unknown. There is a legend that they were gifted to the city of Cologne by Emperor Wilhelm II.. However, there is also a legend that the city donated them in honour of the Emperor.
