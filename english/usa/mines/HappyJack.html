Geology

Chalk is a special kind of limestone, which is porous and soft, and typically very white. It can be used of colour, cement, neutralizing acids in agriculture and of course as building stone. The chalk is diatomaceous sediment, plankton which deposited on the seabed.

Description

Happy Jack Peak and Chalk Mine, or sometime simpler Happy Jack Chalk Mine is a unique show mine in the U.S.A., as it is actually the only room and pillar chalk mine in North America. The chalk hill Happy Jack Peak (624 m asl) is located at Highway 11, north of Saint Paul. The trapper, guide and government scout Jack Swearengen lived in a dugout in the hill, when the first settler arrived in 1872. The hill soon became known as Happy Jack Peak, and it was an important lookout to detect surprise Indian attacks. Mining started with Ed Wright in 1877, who used the stone to build a general store in 1887. Most houses were erected with chalk foundations, though the houses were made of wood. Then the mine was abandoned for decades until the 1930s, when it was bought and reopend by a paint company from Omaha. They extracted chalk for paint filler, makeup, chicken feed and much more.

In the 1960s a local bought the mine and opened it for the public. In 1967 the mine was purchased by the Nebraska State Game and Parks Commission for use as a wayside park. Its unclear why the mine was closed in the 1970s, probably the man simply retired. When a Boy Scout group cleaned up the site in 1995, some locals had the idea to reopen the show mine. This reopening was done by volunteers but with community involvement. In 1997 the Happy Jack Chalk Mine Association renovated the mine and added lights. They built stairs to the summit of Happy Jack’s Peak and a museum with displays of mining equipment. Since then it is open to the public, though it was closed in 2024 for renovation.
