Quarta Colônia UNESCO Global Geopark


The geopark is located in the state of Rio Grande do Sul, in the southernmost tip of Brazil. In 2005 it was created as a State Park named Parque Estadual Quarta Colônia (Quarta Colônia State Park). This was a result of the construction of the Dona Francisca Hydroelectric Plant on the Jacuí River. The park was environmental compensation and intended to protect the forest remnants and local species. At some point This geopark has a lot of paleontological heritage, sedimentary rocks between 233 and 225 Ma from the Triassic Period, contain remains of dinosaurs. It actually documents the rise of one of the most famous animal groups in Paleontology. Despite this emphasis on paleontolgy, there are numerous geosites which are caves or gorges. Unfortunately we have them not listed. The reason is simple, they do not qualify as actual tourist sites, and we avoid to list sites which are too dangerous or difficult. Nevertheless, we decided to mention a few here. If you are not put off by our statement, you may check the official website of the geopark for more details.

Cânions da Piruva (Piruva Canyons) is a narrow gorge with up to 250 m high vertical walls. Unfortunately it is not developed, and there is a river flowing through, so a visit is actually canyoning. There is a local operator who offers such tours, and that’s actually the only way to get there, because it is private property and he is the only one who the permission. (-29.472616, -53.623633)

Gruta do Índio (Indian Cave) is an erosional cave in sandstone, eroded by a meander of a river which has since cut deeper. The sandstone is Lower Cretaceous Serra Geral Formation. The cave is quite spacious, has an area of about 500 m². The entrance portal is 15 m wide and 8 m high. (-29.5389844, -53.1138415)

Toca da Tigra (Tigress's Den) is a rather small cave, only 15 m long with a triangular portal 4 m wide and 3 m high. There are more, even smaller, caves around, which are unnamed. The caves are erosional caves in sandstone, which were created by a meander of the river, which has cut deeper since then. (-29.310927, -53.265026)
